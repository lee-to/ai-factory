# English editorial rules

Use only after activation and artifact-language resolution in
`../WRITING-PROFILE.md`. These adapted rules are inspired by ASD-STE100;
they do not establish compliance with the standard or its dictionary.

- Classify passages as instructions or descriptions. Keep notes descriptive.
- For existing authorized instructions, prefer one action per sentence and
  active voice. Put the condition before its action while retaining its scope.
  Do not convert permissions, recommendations, or descriptions into commands.
- Aim for at most 20 words in procedural sentences and 25 in descriptions.
  Split only when the condition, actor, exception, and order stay clear.
- Give one new fact per sentence and one topic per paragraph. Prefer at most
  six sentences per paragraph. Keep grammar complete; expand contractions
  outside protected text. Clarify pronoun referents without inventing actors.
- Break long noun chains with prepositions when this preserves project terms.
  Prefer direct verbs and familiar words over empty nominal constructions.
- Remove filler only when it carries no fact, limitation, or uncertainty.
  Keep modal verbs and RFC 2119 terms; never apply an automatic modal ladder.
- Keep established warning levels and the risk statement. Place warnings before
  the affected step without changing the authorized action or adding hazards.
- Check the three longest sentences, conditions, pronouns, and terminology.
  A style exception is preferable to a semantic change. Compare every changed
  passage with its source before reporting the result.

Code, commands, identifiers, quoted errors, logs, and other protected spans
remain exact. Project terminology takes priority over synonym reduction.
